**For:** consultants and executives who travel for client work, board meetings, or roadshows

*If you travel for client work, board meetings, or roadshows and end up reconstructing a spreadsheet of receipts from memory a week later — this is for you.*

<Callout type="note">
**In short:** start a Trip in Dori, drop receipts as they happen (photo, forward, or upload), and Dori tracks the running total and status — draft, submitted, paid — right through to reimbursement, scoped to the account it belongs to.
</Callout>

Business travel racks up expenses fast — flights, hotels, meals, local transport — and by the time you're back at your desk, half the receipts are crumpled in a bag or buried in an email inbox. Reconstructing an expense report after the fact is tedious and error-prone — 30-45 minutes you'll never get back, guessing at what a crumpled receipt was even for.

## How a Trip tracks itself

A **Trip** in Dori is a running record of everything you spend on that trip, tied to the client or account it's billable to. Drop receipts as you go — Dori tracks the receipt count and running total — and when you're done, submit it for reimbursement.

![Trips Expense Tracker & Client Reimbursement Dashboard](/images/guides/trips-dashboard.png)

## What's actually driving this, and what it costs

Receipt logging, running totals, and status tracking are handled locally by Dori's engine at zero API cost. Reading text off a receipt image, if needed, is the one part that may use your connected [AI provider](/getting-started/choose-your-ai-provider).

## Trip status, start to finish

- **Draft** — the trip is ongoing; receipts are still being added.
- **Submitted** — you've sent it for reimbursement.
- **Paid** — reimbursement complete.
- **Needs attention** — something doesn't reconcile (for example, the trip was marked resolved while still in draft) and needs a look before it can close.

## Quick setup

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start a Trip">
    In Dori Desktop, go to **Trips** and start one — give it a name and link it to the account it's billable to (or mark it personal).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Drop receipts as you go">
    Photograph or forward receipts as they happen instead of collecting paper. Each one adds to the trip's receipt count and running total.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit when you're back">
    Once travel is done, submit the trip. Its status moves from draft to submitted to paid as reimbursement goes through.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Why this beats a spreadsheet

- **No end-of-trip reconstruction**: Receipts are logged when they happen, not remembered a week later.
- **Scoped to the right account**: A trip billable to a client stays linked to that account, not mixed in with your personal spend.
- **Status you can actually see**: Draft, submitted, paid, or needs-attention — not a guess about where reimbursement stands.
